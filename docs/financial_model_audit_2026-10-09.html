Financial model verification

FINANCE_DASHBOARD · 9 October 2026 · source observations retrieved during this audit · corrections applied locally

The dashboard is useful as a research monitor, but the headline probabilities are not reliably calibrated. Several definite calculation and presentation errors were corrected. The revised-data walk-forward exercise still produces large false alarms and little improvement over an expanding historical base rate. Passing software tests does not establish financial forecasting accuracy.

What was verified

Reviewed recession targets, feature engineering, estimation, aggregation, uncertainty, labor transforms, Treasury inversion statistics, GDP accounting, credit composites, policy-rate distributions, CAPE/return arithmetic, and AI price indicators. Retrieved observations and metadata for 92 FRED series, checked the 311,164-row policy export and 1,750-row CAPE file, independently compared CAPE with Shiller's current workbook, and ran synthetic regression checks plus the complete test suite. This is a source and computation audit, not an independent certification of upstream economic statistics or every watchlist research claim.

Observed data issues and checks that passed

  • FRED: all 92 series loaded during the audit; zero duplicate observation dates or nonfinite numeric observations. Frequency metadata matches the corrected probit configuration. Successful loading does not mean every date exists or every source is current.
  • Unemployment gap: October 2025 is missing. BLS confirms it was not collected during the shutdown. This propagates into derived comparisons; no unemployment value was invented. Published Sahm readings remain available. Where a full reconstruction is possible, 895 months match the published current-vintage indicator within 0.00334pp, its rounding tolerance.
  • Claims source inconsistency: 7 of 3,115 overlapping historical weeks differ between recomputed four-week ICSA and IC4WSA; largest difference 18,500 on 7 January 2012. Recent observations match. This is an upstream historical discrepancy requiring source/vintage investigation, not proof that one series is corrupt.
  • History restrictions: ICE HY and IG spreads currently begin 9 October 2023; FRED S&P 500 begins October 2016; USSLIND ends February 2020. These limits materially affect long-history percentile and composite comparisons.
  • CAPE: all 1,750 bundled months match Shiller's current official workbook: maximum CAPE difference 7.1×10−15, maximum relative real-total-return-price difference 2.2×10−16. Latest CAPE 40.7106. The workbook notes that October uses the October 2 close and estimated September/October CPI. Current-month CAPE is now labeled provisional.
  • Policy export: 887 snapshots through 7 October 2026; no duplicate date/quarter/field keys, no reversed interquartile bounds, probabilities between 0 and 100, and no bucket sums above 100.1%. Missing mass reflects an incomplete export; its original data were preserved.
  • Manual AI research spot checks: SpaceX's SPCX ticker and June 12 listing date match its IPO announcement; CoreWeave's 67% Microsoft share of FY2025 revenue matches its 10-K; NVIDIA's $108.5bn maximum gross guarantee exposure matches its Q2 FY2027 filing. These facts are not estimates of expected losses. Other manually maintained figures and planned event dates were not independently certified in this round.

Remaining limitations and next research work

  1. Calibration and model concentration: all three ensemble members share the yield curve; averaging them does not create independent evidence. Consider coefficient regularization and a genuinely different predictor family, with calibration evaluated inside nested time splits or held-out cycles. Do not optimize against the 2022–2024 false alarm and then call the result out-of-sample.
  2. Real-time evaluation: use ALFRED vintages, actual release calendars, and NBER announcement availability. Current scores remain a revised-data diagnostic, not demonstrated deployable forecasting skill. The live fit currently uses features at their reference dates, while walk-forward fitting shifts them by approximate release lags; those time alignments differ. The headline horizon is anchored to its common reference month (August 2026), not automatically to the runtime date (October 9). Align live and historical forecasting to an explicit information-as-of date before interpreting the headline as a fresh next-year forecast.
  3. Uncertainty: the BIC pairs bootstrap resamples monthly rows independently and conditions on today's selected specification. Overlapping labels, serial dependence, selection uncertainty and regime changes remain outside the interval. Evaluate moving-block bootstrap choices and cycle-level sensitivity. Four evaluation cycles are a severe evidence limit.
  4. Composite comparability: growth and credit factors standardize each component over its own full available history. Historical readings use later data, and component coverage changes over time. Credit OAS normalization now spans only three years while other inputs span decades. Choose and disclose a common reference period or expanding normalization before treating historical percentiles as comparable. Retail sales are nominal while consumption is real.
  5. Judgmental indicators: composite weights, severity bands, AI risk scores and alert thresholds have not been empirically validated as probabilities or trading rules. Current-constituent baskets have survivorship bias; daily rebalancing assumes frictionless trading; local-currency proxy returns exclude FX and dividends. Add per-signal freshness and coverage gates before relying on stale or thin feeds.
  6. Provenance: attach filing links and as-of dates to every quantitative AI exposure note; retain source release/vintage metadata; automate validation of incomplete policy exports and restricted histories.

Effect on today's readings and reproducibility

Using the same saved source observations, the recession headline changed from 10.47% to 11.87%. The corrected members, all scored for August 2026, are spread-only 13.47%, Wright-style 6.67%, and BIC-selected 15.47%. The BIC features remain SPREAD and new-home-sales YoY; its current probability did not change. Separate benchmarks are Estrella–Mishkin 9.53% for month 12 and Chauvet–Piger 0.62% for the coincident state. These values answer different questions and should not be averaged.
